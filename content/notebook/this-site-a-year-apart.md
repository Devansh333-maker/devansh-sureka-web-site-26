A year ago I built my first website. It took me thirty full days. I came from a non-tech background, the AI boom had only just started, and I was figuring out every step as I went: which tool to use, how to ask for what I wanted, and why it kept breaking.

This year I built it again. AI has moved on a long way since then, and so have I. This version took about three full days of work. I did it in pieces, around everything else, so it stretched across a week.

Same person, same work, a year apart. Here they are on top of each other.

[compare: then-now]

## What changed

Then, the site told you what I do. Now it tries to show it: an island you can walk around, a place for each kind of work, and numbers instead of adjectives.

Then, I picked a template and filled it in. Now every part is shaped by what it holds: a magazine you can page through, water drawn to scale, a carousel of posters, a guide who walks the page with you.

Then, a visitor had to read headings to find their way. Now the aim is that a stranger understands who I am, what I have done and what I can do within thirty seconds.

## What it took

The first time, most of the thirty days went into learning what was even possible. This time I knew what I wanted, and the tools could keep up. The hard part was no longer building it. It was deciding what was worth building.

> Thirty days to learn how. Three days to do it better.

The first post in this notebook is about how that first version was made. This one is about how far a year can take you.
